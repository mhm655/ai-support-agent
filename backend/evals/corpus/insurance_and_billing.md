# Brightleaf Family Dental - Insurance, Payment and Billing

## Plans we are in network with

Brightleaf Family Dental is in network with the following dental plans:

- Delta Dental PPO and Delta Dental Premier
- Cigna DPPO
- MetLife PDP Plus
- Guardian PPO
- Aetna Dental PPO
- United Concordia Advantage Plus
- Ameritas PPO
- Principal PPO

Being in network means we have agreed a discounted fee with that carrier and
we bill them directly. You pay only your share, which is usually a copay,
your deductible if it has not been met, and any percentage the plan does not
cover.

## Plans we are not in network with

We are not in network with Humana dental plans, Careington discount plans,
or any Medicaid or state-funded dental program. We are also not in network
with dental HMO or DMO products from any carrier, including the DHMO
products sold by carriers whose PPO plans we do accept. Cigna DHMO is a
common example: we take Cigna DPPO but not Cigna DHMO.

We do not accept Medicaid, NC Health Choice, or any state dental assistance
program at either office. We are sorry to turn people away for this and we
keep a printed list of nearby practices that do accept Medicaid at the front
desk of both offices.

Medicare does not include routine dental coverage, so Medicare patients are
treated as self-pay unless they carry a separate dental policy or a Medicare
Advantage plan with a dental rider. If you have a Medicare Advantage plan,
bring the card and we will check the dental benefit before your visit.

## Out of network benefits

If your plan is not on the in-network list you may still have out of network
benefits. In that case we collect the full self-pay fee at the time of the
visit and give you a completed claim form to submit to your insurer for
reimbursement. We do not submit out of network claims on your behalf. The
reimbursement goes to you, not to us.

## Verifying your coverage

We verify benefits for every new patient before the first appointment, and
again in January for existing patients, because plans change at the start of
the calendar year more often than people expect. Verification is an estimate
of coverage, not a guarantee of payment. The final amount is determined by
the carrier when the claim is processed.

To have your benefits verified, call the front desk at least two business
days before your appointment with the subscriber name, date of birth, member
ID and the employer or group name.

## Deductibles and annual maximums

Most dental plans carry an annual maximum, commonly between one thousand and
two thousand dollars per person per year, and most reset on January 1 rather
than on the anniversary of your enrollment. Unused benefit does not carry
over. Patients with large treatment plans often split the work across two
calendar years to use two annual maximums, and we will help plan the timing
if that is useful.

Deductibles are usually fifty dollars per person per year for in-network
care, and are typically waived for preventive visits such as cleanings,
exams and x-rays. Most plans cover preventive care at one hundred percent,
basic work such as fillings at eighty percent, and major work such as crowns
at fifty percent, but your own plan may differ.

## What you pay at the visit

Your estimated share is due at the time of service. We accept cash, personal
checks, all major credit and debit cards, health savings account and flexible
spending account cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay.

We do not accept payment in cryptocurrency, and we cannot take payment over
the phone for a balance under twenty five dollars.

## Payment plans and financing

For treatment plans over one thousand dollars we offer an in-house payment
plan: twenty five percent down, then the balance over six monthly
installments with no interest and no credit check. The plan is set up at the
front desk and requires a card kept on file for the monthly charge.

For larger cases we work with CareCredit, which offers longer terms including
twelve and twenty four month interest-free options if the balance is paid
within the promotional period. CareCredit is a third party lender and the
application is a soft credit check to start.

Orthodontic treatment has its own arrangement: a down payment of nine hundred
dollars, then equal monthly payments across the length of treatment, which is
typically eighteen to twenty four months.

## Billing questions and disputes

Statements go out on the first of each month for any balance remaining after
insurance has paid. Balances are due within thirty days of the statement
date. Accounts unpaid after ninety days may be referred to a collections
agency, though we will always attempt to reach you by phone and email first.

If you believe a charge is wrong, call the billing line at (919) 555-0148 and
ask for a claim review. We will pull the claim, the carrier's explanation of
benefits, and the clinical note, and walk through them with you. Most billing
disputes turn out to be a mismatch between what the plan estimated and what
it actually paid, and in many of those cases the carrier can be appealed
successfully.

Refunds for overpayment are issued within fourteen business days, back to the
original payment method where possible.
