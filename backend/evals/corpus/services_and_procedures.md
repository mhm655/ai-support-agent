# Brightleaf Family Dental - Services and What to Expect

## Cleanings and preventive visits

A routine cleaning appointment takes about forty five minutes and is done by
a hygienist. It includes scaling to remove plaque and tartar, polishing, and
a check of your gum measurements. A dentist looks in at the end of the visit
for the exam. Most adults are seen every six months; patients with a history
of gum disease are usually seen every three or four months instead.

Fluoride varnish takes about two minutes and is painted on at the end. You
should avoid hot drinks and hard or crunchy food for four hours afterward,
and avoid brushing until the next morning so the varnish stays in contact
with the enamel.

Sealants are a thin coating placed in the grooves of the back teeth, most
often for children shortly after the permanent molars come in, around age
six and again around age twelve. Placing them is painless and requires no
numbing.

## Fillings

A filling appointment takes between thirty and sixty minutes depending on
how many surfaces are involved. The tooth is numbed with a local anesthetic,
the decay is removed, and the space is filled with a tooth-coloured composite
resin that is hardened with a curing light.

You can eat as soon as the numbness wears off, which usually takes two to
three hours. There is no waiting period with composite fillings, unlike the
older amalgam material which needed time to set. Some sensitivity to cold
for a week or two afterward is normal and is not a sign anything has gone
wrong. Sensitivity that is getting worse after two weeks, or pain when
biting down, should be reported to us.

## Crowns

A conventional crown takes two visits about three weeks apart. At the first
visit the tooth is shaped, an impression or digital scan is taken, and a
temporary crown is cemented. At the second visit the temporary comes off and
the permanent crown is fitted and cemented. Each visit is about ninety
minutes.

Same-day crowns are milled in the office from a ceramic block while you wait.
The appointment runs about two and a half hours from start to finish, and
there is no temporary and no second visit.

While wearing a temporary crown, avoid chewing gum, caramel, toffee and other
sticky foods, which can pull the temporary off, and chew on the other side
where you can. If a temporary does come off, keep it and call us; it is not
an emergency but it should be recemented within a few days so the tooth does
not shift.

## Root canal treatment

A root canal removes infected or inflamed pulp from inside the tooth. Front
teeth usually take one visit of about an hour. Molars have more canals and
usually take either one longer visit of ninety minutes to two hours, or two
shorter visits.

The procedure itself is done under local anesthetic and most patients report
it feels much like having a filling. The pain people associate with root
canals is almost always the infection beforehand, not the treatment. Mild
soreness for two to three days afterward is normal and responds well to
ibuprofen.

A tooth that has had a root canal becomes more brittle and nearly always
needs a crown afterward to prevent it fracturing. We usually place the crown
two to four weeks after the root canal.

## Extractions

A simple extraction of a fully erupted tooth takes about twenty to thirty
minutes. A surgical extraction, where the tooth is broken up or gum tissue
is lifted, takes longer, typically forty five minutes to an hour.

Afterward, bite firmly on the gauze for thirty to forty five minutes. Do not
rinse forcefully, do not use a straw, and do not smoke for at least
seventy two hours, because the suction can dislodge the clot and cause a dry
socket, which is painful and needs a follow-up visit to dress. Eat soft food
for the first day and keep to lukewarm rather than hot food.

Some swelling peaking around forty eight hours after the extraction is
expected. Swelling that is still increasing on day four, fever, or a bad
taste with worsening pain should be reported to us the same day.

## Wisdom teeth

We remove erupted wisdom teeth in the office at the Riverside location. Fully
impacted wisdom teeth, or cases needing general anesthetic or IV sedation,
are referred to an oral surgeon we work with, whose office is a ten minute
drive from Riverside.

## Implants

An implant replaces a missing tooth with a titanium fixture placed in the
jawbone, an abutment on top of it, and a crown on top of that. The fixture
needs three to six months to integrate with the bone before the crown goes
on, which is why the whole process runs across several months.

Not everyone is a candidate. Adequate bone volume is needed, and uncontrolled
diabetes or heavy smoking substantially raises the failure rate. We take a
3D scan before recommending an implant.

## Gum disease treatment

Scaling and root planing is a deep cleaning below the gum line, done one
quadrant at a time under local anesthetic, usually across two visits of two
quadrants each. Afterward the gums are tender for a few days and may bleed
when brushing, which settles within a week.

## Sedation and anxiety

We offer nitrous oxide, sometimes called laughing gas, at both offices at
ninety five dollars per visit. It wears off within minutes and you can drive
yourself home afterward.

Oral sedation with a tablet taken before the appointment is available at the
Riverside office only, costs two hundred and forty dollars, and requires
someone to drive you both ways. We do not offer IV sedation or general
anesthetic in either office and refer those cases out.

If dental anxiety is the reason you have not been seen in a while, tell us
when booking. We will schedule a longer first visit with no treatment
planned, so the first appointment is only a conversation and a look.

## Night guards and grinding

A custom night guard for grinding, also called bruxism, is four hundred and
twenty dollars and takes two visits: a scan, then a fitting about two weeks
later. Over the counter boil-and-bite guards are cheaper but wear through
quickly and fit poorly, and we do not recommend them for heavy grinders.
