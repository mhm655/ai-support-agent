# Brightleaf Family Dental - Dentures, Partials and Appliances

## Complete dentures

A complete denture replaces all the teeth in one arch. The process takes
four to five appointments across six to eight weeks: impressions, a bite
registration, a wax try-in where you see and approve the tooth arrangement
before anything is finalised, and the fit.

A complete upper or lower denture is one thousand eight hundred and fifty
dollars per arch. A full set, upper and lower together, is three thousand
four hundred dollars.

The wax try-in stage matters. That is the point at which the shape, shade and
arrangement of the teeth can still be changed freely, and changes after the
denture is processed are far more limited and may incur a remake fee.

## Immediate dentures

An immediate denture is made in advance and placed on the same day the
remaining teeth are extracted, so you are never without teeth. It costs two
thousand two hundred dollars per arch.

The trade-off is that the gum shrinks substantially as it heals over the
following three to six months, so an immediate denture will need relining,
and often a full remake, once healing is complete. The reline is not included
in the initial fee and is three hundred and eighty dollars.

## Partial dentures

A partial denture replaces some teeth and clasps onto the remaining ones. An
acrylic partial is nine hundred and fifty dollars per arch. A cast metal
framework partial, which is thinner, stronger and considerably more
comfortable, is two thousand one hundred dollars per arch. A flexible nylon
partial is one thousand four hundred dollars.

Cast metal partials last far longer than acrylic ones and are what we
recommend for a long-term partial. Acrylic partials are best treated as a
transitional appliance while healing or while saving for an implant.

## Implant-retained overdentures

A lower denture that clips onto two implants is dramatically more stable than
one that rests on the gum, and this is the single change most often
transformative for people struggling with a loose lower denture. Two
implants plus the attachments and the overdenture is six thousand eight
hundred dollars in total.

Lower dentures are much less stable than upper ones, because an upper denture
gets suction from the palate and a lower one has only the ridge and has to
compete with the tongue. That is why almost all denture complaints we hear
are about the lower.

## Living with a denture

New dentures feel bulky and cause increased saliva for the first week or two,
and that settles. Expect two or three adjustment visits in the first month
for sore spots; these are included at no charge for ninety days after fit.

Take dentures out at night. The tissue underneath needs time without
pressure, and wearing them around the clock raises the risk of denture
stomatitis, a fungal inflammation of the palate.

Clean dentures with a soft brush and soap or a denture cleaner, never with
regular toothpaste, which is abrasive and scratches the acrylic. Store them
in water overnight so the acrylic does not dry and warp. Never use hot water,
which will distort them permanently.

Dentures should be relined every two to three years, because the ridge
underneath continues to resorb for as long as you wear them. A reline is
three hundred and eighty dollars and a repair to a fractured denture is
between one hundred and eighty and three hundred and twenty dollars
depending on the break.

## Night guards and grinding

A custom hard acrylic night guard is four hundred and twenty dollars and
takes two visits about two weeks apart. It does not stop you grinding; it
protects the teeth from the grinding, which is the realistic goal.

Signs of grinding include waking with jaw ache or a headache at the temples,
flattened or chipped tooth edges, and a partner who reports hearing it.
Grinding is strongly associated with stress and with sleep apnoea, and if
there is loud snoring or witnessed pauses in breathing we will suggest a
sleep assessment rather than just making a guard.

## Sports mouthguards

A custom-made sports mouthguard is one hundred and eighty dollars and takes
a scan and one fitting visit. Boil-and-bite guards from a sports shop are
better than nothing but fit loosely, get chewed through, and are often
spat out during play.

We strongly recommend a custom guard for any contact sport, and for skating
and mountain biking. Replacing a knocked-out front tooth over a lifetime
costs many thousands of dollars; the guard is the cheapest dentistry you will
ever buy.

Children in braces need a specially shaped orthodontic mouthguard, which is
two hundred and ten dollars, and it will need remaking as the teeth move.

## Bridges versus implants versus partials

For a single missing tooth there are three realistic options. An implant
costs more up front at four thousand three hundred and forty dollars total,
but does not involve cutting down the neighbouring teeth and typically lasts
longest. A three-unit bridge is three thousand six hundred dollars, is faster
at about three weeks, but requires preparing the two adjacent teeth, and if
one of them later fails the whole bridge is lost. A partial denture is the
cheapest but is removable and least comfortable.

We will lay out all three with costs rather than steering you, because the
right answer genuinely depends on the condition of the neighbouring teeth,
on bone volume, and on what you want to spend.
