# Brightleaf Family Dental - Children and Orthodontics

## When to bring a child for the first time

The recommendation from the American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry is a
first dental visit by the first birthday, or within six months of the first
tooth appearing, whichever comes first. That first visit is short and mostly
a look and a conversation with the parent about feeding, brushing and
fluoride.

We see children from age one at the Oak Park office with Dr. Raman, and
older children at either office.

## Children's cleanings

A child cleaning is ninety five dollars for patients twelve and under and
takes about thirty minutes. Fluoride varnish is applied at most children's
visits and is included at no extra charge for patients under sixteen, unlike
the adult fee of forty five dollars.

Sealants are placed on permanent molars, usually around age six and again
around age twelve when the second molars come through. Sealants are sixty
dollars per tooth and most insurance plans cover them fully for children
under fourteen.

## Nervous children

Dr. Raman uses a tell-show-do approach: explain the instrument, show it, then
use it. For a genuinely frightened child we will happily spend the first
appointment doing nothing more than sitting in the chair and counting teeth,
and book the actual treatment for another day. We do not use physical
restraint, and we do not hold children down.

Nitrous oxide is available for children at both offices at ninety five
dollars per visit and is generally very well tolerated from about age four.
Children who need full treatment under general anesthetic are referred to
the hospital dental service, and that referral typically has a wait of three
to five months.

## Thumb sucking and pacifiers

Thumb sucking and pacifier use are normal in infancy and most children stop
on their own between two and four years of age. It becomes worth actively
addressing if it continues past about age four, or once the permanent front
teeth start to come in, because sustained pressure at that stage can move
the teeth and change the shape of the palate.

## Teething

Teething typically starts between four and seven months. Drooling,
irritability and wanting to chew on things are normal. A fever over one
hundred point four degrees, diarrhoea, or a rash are not caused by teething
and should be assessed by a pediatrician rather than attributed to it.

Chilled, not frozen, teething rings help. Do not use topical teething gels
containing benzocaine in children under two, and avoid amber teething
necklaces entirely, which are a strangulation and choking risk.

## Orthodontic assessment

We recommend an orthodontic assessment around age seven. That is early
enough to spot crossbites, crowding and jaw growth problems where a short
early intervention can avoid longer treatment later, and it does not mean
braces go on at seven. Most children who are assessed at seven are simply
monitored until the permanent teeth are through.

The orthodontic consultation is free at both offices, and includes a
panoramic x-ray and a scan.

## Braces and aligners

Traditional fixed braces cost five thousand four hundred dollars for a full
course of treatment, and clear aligner treatment is five thousand nine
hundred dollars. Limited treatment on a single arch, or minor correction, is
three thousand two hundred dollars.

Treatment typically runs eighteen to twenty four months for a full case, with
adjustment appointments every six to eight weeks for fixed braces, or
check-ins every ten to twelve weeks for aligners.

Aligners only work if they are worn. They need to be in the mouth twenty two
hours a day, coming out only for eating and brushing. Patients who cannot
commit to that do better with fixed braces, and we will say so at the
consultation rather than sell an aligner case that will not work.

## Life with braces

Avoid hard, sticky and chewy food: caramel, toffee, hard candy, nuts, popcorn
kernels, and ice. Cut apples and carrots into pieces rather than biting into
them whole. A broken bracket is not an emergency but does need an
appointment, because time in a broken appliance is time not spent moving
teeth.

Brush after every meal with braces on, and use the interdental brushes we
supply. Decalcification, the white square marks left on the enamel where a
bracket sat, is permanent, and is caused by plaque left around the brackets
rather than by the braces themselves.

## Retainers

Retainers are not optional and are not temporary. Teeth move for life, and
without a retainer they will drift back toward where they started, usually
within the first year. We fit a fixed wire behind the lower front teeth in
most cases, plus a removable retainer for night wear.

A removable retainer is three hundred and forty dollars per arch and a
replacement is two hundred and ninety dollars. The single most common way
retainers are lost is being wrapped in a napkin at a restaurant, so keep the
case with you and use it.

Wear the removable retainer every night for the first year, then most nights
indefinitely. If a retainer suddenly feels tight, that means the teeth have
already started to move, and you should wear it more, not less, and call us.
